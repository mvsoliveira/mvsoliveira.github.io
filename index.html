From Três Rios to Geneva, and on to silicon

20 years of study and work, drawn the way I look at signals all day. Studies and jobs ran in parallel for most of it.

The copper cursor marks today.

I grew up in Três Rios, in the state of Rio de Janeiro, and went to school on a scholarship.

In Juiz de Fora I attended high school in the morning and a technical course in electrical engineering at night, walking 8 to 10 km a day to save on bus tickets. At university I did not miss a class. I knew the chance was a rare one.

At 23, while finishing my degree, I was invited to CERN for one year. It was my first flight and my first time outside Brazil. In my first meetings I understood about five words in two hours.

I stayed for 15 years. While working on the ATLAS trigger I completed a master's at UFJF and a PhD at EPFL, and came to supervise students of my own.

Then I left the public sector for the private one, to design chips at Netpreme.

Three theses

Each one in two versions. Pick the one you want to read.

2016–2021PhD, EPFL

Low-Latency High-Bandwidth Circuit and System Design for Trigger Systems in High Energy Physics

I designed the prototype of a new muon processor for ATLAS and invented a way of sorting fast enough for it: 352 items in 31.25 nanoseconds, about 12 million times faster than the blink of an eye.

Low-latency, fixed-latency synchronisation of 208 high-speed serial inputs, bit-error-rate qualification of every link at 12.8 Gb/s, and a 352-input sorting network implemented in both RTL and high-level synthesis with a latency of 31.25 ns.

Defended before a jury chaired by Prof. Giovanni De Micheli. Among the examiners was Prof. Sherenaz Al-Haj Baddar, author of the fastest known 22-key sorting network, from which my 352-key network is built.

2012–2015Master, UFJF

The ATLAS Level-1 Muon Topological Trigger Information for Run 2 of the LHC

I upgraded the muon electronics of ATLAS so that the experiment could use the position of each muon when choosing which collisions have the most potential for science.

Firmware upgrade of the ATLAS Muon-to-Central-Trigger-Processor Interface (MUCTPI): sorting, encoding and serialising the two highest-momentum muon candidates per sector at 320 Mb/s over outputs never meant for it, validated with phase scans and bit-error-rate tests.

2006–2011Bachelor, UFJF

Timing Synchronization Techniques for OFDM Systems

Before a digital receiver can read anything, it has to find exactly where each block of data begins. I compared techniques for doing that, proposed a cheaper one, and built them in hardware.

Comparison of preamble- and cyclic-prefix-based timing estimators for OFDM, a proposed low-complexity estimator (MMAE) for real-valued HS-OFDM, and recursive FPGA implementations of all of them.
